# 2.3.2 Three Flashes

WCAG 2.2 · Level **AAA**

A practical summary in our own words; the exact obligations are set by the normative text and its definitions.

## Applicability

Flashes in content; AAA.

## What is required

No more than three flashes in any 1-second period.

## How to test

1. Check the frequency in all dynamic material and transitions.

**Expected result:** No more than three flashes in any 1-second period. All applicable states and normative exceptions have been checked.

**Fails when:** The frequency exceeds three flashes per second, even over a small area.

## How to fix

Replace the effect or reduce the frequency.

## Example

**Before:** An indicator blinks rapidly five times a second.

**After:** The indicator does not blink.

## Exceptions and false positives

Unlike 2.3.1, the "below the area/luminance threshold" exception does not allow more frequent flashes here.

## Automation and evidence

A tool can collect candidates and measurements; the verdict depends on applicability, content, exceptions and observed behavior. The absence of an automated signal is not a pass. The main conclusion requires a person to carry out the scenario, or an observed interactive run; a static DOM alone is not enough.

- URL/route, interface state, date, sign-in method and the steps tested.
- Viewport in CSS px, zoom level, input method, user agent, and assistive technologies if used.
- The specific element/media segment/process step, reproducible observations and the actual result.
- The normative condition of 2.3.2, the exceptions checked, and an explanation of the barrier to the user.
- For this test, record: Check the frequency in all dynamic material and transitions.

## Code patterns

The scenario is given above. Code alone is not enough to test media, processes and semantics.
