# 3.1.6 Pronunciation

WCAG 2.2 · Level **AAA**

A practical summary in our own words; the exact obligations are set by the normative text and its definitions.

## Applicability

The meaning of a word in context is ambiguous without knowing its pronunciation; AAA.

## What is required

Provide a mechanism for identifying the correct pronunciation.

## How to test

1. Find ambiguous words.
2. Check that the pronunciation is explained in text or in audio.

**Expected result:** Provide a mechanism for identifying the correct pronunciation. All applicable states and normative exceptions have been checked.

**Fails when:** The meaning of a key word depends on stress that cannot be determined from the context.

## How to fix

Add a stress mark, a phonetic explanation or accessible audio.

## Example

**Before:** A learning exercise uses the ambiguous word "record" without a hint.

**After:** The stress and the meaning are clarified.

## Exceptions and false positives

Do not require pronunciation for every word; the meaning must be ambiguous without the pronunciation.

## Automation and evidence

A tool can collect candidates and measurements; the verdict depends on applicability, content, exceptions and observed behavior. The absence of an automated signal is not a pass. The main conclusion requires a person to carry out the scenario, or an observed interactive run; a static DOM alone is not enough.

- URL/route, interface state, date, sign-in method and the steps tested.
- Viewport in CSS px, zoom level, input method, user agent, and assistive technologies if used.
- The specific element/media segment/process step, reproducible observations and the actual result.
- The normative condition of 3.1.6, the exceptions checked, and an explanation of the barrier to the user.
- For this test, record: Find ambiguous words; check that the pronunciation is explained in text or in audio.

## Code patterns

The scenario is given above. Code alone is not enough to test media, processes and semantics.
