# 2.4.3 Focus Order

WCAG 2.2 · Level **A**

A practical summary in our own words; the exact obligations are set by the normative text and its definitions.

## Applicability

Sequential navigation affects meaning or operation.

## What is required

The focus order preserves meaning and operability.

## How to test

1. Move forward and backward.
2. Open a dialog, delete an item and refresh a list.
3. Record the actual focus path.

**Expected result:** The focus order preserves meaning and operability. All applicable states and normative exceptions have been checked.

**Fails when:** After a form opens, focus lands behind it, which disrupts the task.

## How to fix

Align the DOM with the focus order; manage focus sensibly on dynamic changes.

## Example

**Before:** After a row is added, focus is lost to the top of the page.

**After:** Focus stays at the expected point to continue from.

## Exceptions and false positives

The order does not have to match the visual order literally, as long as meaning and operation are preserved. A positive tabindex is a risk signal, not a failure in itself.

## Automation and evidence

A tool can collect candidates and measurements; the verdict depends on applicability, content, exceptions and observed behavior. The absence of an automated signal is not a pass. The main conclusion requires a person to carry out the scenario, or an observed interactive run; a static DOM alone is not enough.

- URL/route, interface state, date, sign-in method and the steps tested.
- Viewport in CSS px, zoom level, input method, user agent, and assistive technologies if used.
- The specific element/media segment/process step, reproducible observations and the actual result.
- The normative condition of 2.4.3, the exceptions checked, and an explanation of the barrier to the user.
- For this test, record: Move forward and backward; open a dialog, delete an item and refresh a list; record the actual focus path.
