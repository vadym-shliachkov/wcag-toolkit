# 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast

WCAG 2.2 · Level **AA**

A practical summary in our own words; the exact obligations are set by the normative text and its definitions.

## Applicability

Visual indicators of components and their states, and meaningful parts of graphics.

## What is required

The parts needed for identification have a contrast of at least 3:1 against adjacent colors.

## How to test

1. Determine which border, icon or area is needed for identification.
2. Measure the adjacent colors.
3. Check the states.

**Expected result:** The parts needed for identification have a contrast of at least 3:1 against adjacent colors. All applicable states and normative exceptions have been checked.

**Fails when:** The only border of a field has a contrast of 1.6:1, and the field cannot be distinguished.

## How to fix

Strengthen the necessary border, icon or other identifying cue.

## Example

**Before:** A toggle's state is visible only from a faint dot.

**After:** The dot and the outline have sufficient contrast.

## Exceptions and false positives

Inactive components and unmodified user agent presentation are excluded; graphics have an exception for essential presentation. Not every decorative border has to reach 3:1.

## Automation and evidence

A tool can collect candidates and measurements; the verdict depends on applicability, content, exceptions and observed behavior. The absence of an automated signal is not a pass. For this criterion it is especially important to compare the programmatic data with the actual presentation.

- URL/route, interface state, date, sign-in method and the steps tested.
- Viewport in CSS px, zoom level, input method, user agent, and assistive technologies if used.
- The specific element/media segment/process step, reproducible observations and the actual result.
- The normative condition of 1.4.11, the exceptions checked, and an explanation of the barrier to the user.
- For this test, record: Determine which border, icon or area is needed for identification; measure the adjacent colors; check the states.
