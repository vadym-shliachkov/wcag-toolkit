# 2.5.2 Pointer Cancellation

WCAG 2.2 · Level **A**

A practical summary in our own words; the exact obligations are set by the normative text and its definitions.

## Applicability

Functionality is operated with a single pointer.

## What is required

One branch is enough: the down-event does not execute any part of the function; or completion is on the up-event, with a way to abort before completion or undo after it; or the up-event reverses any outcome of the down-event; or completing on the down-event is essential.

## How to test

1. Press and hold.
2. Move the pointer off the target.
3. Release.
4. Check abort and undo.

**Expected result:** One branch is enough: the down-event does not execute any part of the function; or completion is on the up-event, with a way to abort before completion or undo after it; or the up-event reverses any outcome of the down-event; or completing on the down-event is essential. All applicable states and normative exceptions have been checked.

**Fails when:** The action completes on pointerdown, the up-event does not reverse it, and completing on down is not essential.

## How to fix

Prefer click/up with abort when the pointer moves away; alternatively, implement one of the other normative branches in full.

## Example

**Before:** A card is deleted immediately on touch, with no cancellation on release.

**After:** Releasing outside the button cancels the deletion.

## Exceptions and false positives

Undo alone does not make ordinary completion on the down-event acceptable: the abort/undo branch requires completion on the up-event. Check essentiality separately, for example for key emulation.

## Automation and evidence

A tool can collect candidates and measurements; the verdict depends on applicability, content, exceptions and observed behavior. The absence of an automated signal is not a pass. The main conclusion requires a person to carry out the scenario, or an observed interactive run; a static DOM alone is not enough.

- URL/route, interface state, date, sign-in method and the steps tested.
- Viewport in CSS px, zoom level, input method, user agent, and assistive technologies if used.
- The specific element/media segment/process step, reproducible observations and the actual result.
- The normative condition of 2.5.2, the exceptions checked, and an explanation of the barrier to the user.
- For this test, record: Press and hold; move the pointer off the target; release; check abort and undo.
